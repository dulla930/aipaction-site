KY-04: the $15.5 million operation to replace Thomas Massie

Checked August 20, 2026 · Source: FEC independent-expenditure aggregates for Thomas Massie (H2KY04121) and Ed Gallrein (H6KY04171), 2026 cycle, and FEC Schedule A receipts for MAGA KY (C00908723)

Verdict: Confirmed. $15.5M from three committees.

Our database lists challenger Ed Gallrein at $15,544,205 in Israel lobby money, the largest candidate figure we track. We reproduced that number from raw FEC aggregates. Three committees account for it:

  • United Democracy Project (AIPAC's super PAC): $3,575,107 opposing Massie plus $579,319 supporting Gallrein, $4,154,426 total.
  • RJC Victory Fund (Republican Jewish Coalition): $3,515,615 supporting Gallrein plus $362,844 opposing Massie, $3,878,459 total.
  • MAGA KY: $6,200,914 opposing Massie plus $1,283,199 supporting Gallrein, $7,484,113 total.
  • Combined: $15,516,998 in independent expenditures, matching our database figure within 0.2% (the remainder is direct donations).

"MAGA KY" doesn't sound like an Israel lobby committee. So we pulled its FEC donor filings. Its largest itemized receipts: $1,000,000 from Paul Singer of Elliott Management, among the Republican Jewish Coalition's most prominent backers; $750,000 from Preserve America PAC, the super PAC founded with Adelson money; over $5 million from America 21 PAC across repeated transfers; and $500,000 from MAGA Inc. Judge the label by the funders.

For the record, Massie has his own outside support: Kentucky First PAC ($1.5M for him), Kentucky 4th PAC ($6.7M against Gallrein), Make Liberty Win ($1.1M), and Defeating Communism PAC ($952K). Massie's own career total from pro-Israel PACs remains $0.

Caveats: figures are FEC processed independent-expenditure aggregates as of August 20, 2026, and grow as new filings post. Verify at fec.gov/data: search either candidate under "independent expenditures," and MAGA KY's receipts under committee C00908723.

Michigan Senate: how the $30M primary actually flowed

Checked August 20, 2026 · Source: FEC independent-expenditure aggregates for Haley Stevens (S6MI00426) and Abdul El-Sayed (S6MI00418), 2026 cycle, and FEC Schedule A receipts for A Stronger Michigan (C00952259)

Verdict: Confirmed, with a dark-money middleman.

CBS News reported AIPAC spent roughly $30 million to lift Haley Stevens over Abdul El-Sayed in the August primary. The FEC filings show how the pipeline worked, in two channels:

  • The disclosed channel: United Democracy Project (AIPAC's super PAC) spent $8,437,449 supporting Stevens and $2,346,358 opposing El-Sayed. $10.8M, all under AIPAC's own name.
  • The laundered channel: a super PAC called A Stronger Michigan spent $9,649,850 supporting Stevens. Its FEC receipts show $10.9 million — effectively all of its funding — came from Center Forward, a nonprofit that does not disclose its donors. Center Forward also spent $999,999 directly supporting Stevens.
  • The other side of the ledger: every committee supporting El-Sayed combined, National Nurses United ($490K), Fighting for Michigan PAC ($1.1M), Common Defense ($290K), American Priorities ($154K), and MoveOn ($39K), spent about $2.1M. The pro-Stevens outside money outgunned it roughly 10 to 1.

El-Sayed's own career total from pro-Israel PACs: $0. Stevens' career total in our database, $36.2M+, includes this primary, her 2022 House race, and direct donations.

Caveats: "A Stronger Michigan" totals reflect itemized FEC receipts through the pre-primary filings; Center Forward's own funders are not disclosed anywhere, which is precisely the point. Verify at fec.gov/data under each candidate's independent expenditures and committee C00952259's receipts.

Chicago: AIPAC's super PAC wired $5.3M into two "local" PACs on the same day

Checked August 20, 2026 · Source: FEC independent-expenditure aggregates for IL-08 and IL-02 candidates, and FEC Schedule A receipts for Elect Chicago Women (C00936724), Affordable Chicago Now! (C00935049), and Chicago Progressive Partnership (C00935809)

Verdict: Confirmed. The costume playbook, documented.

In Illinois' open-seat primaries, no pro-Israel committee spent a dollar under its own name. Instead, the FEC filings show this:

  • On February 2, 2026, United Democracy Project, AIPAC's super PAC, wired $4,011,200 to a brand-new PAC called "Elect Chicago Women" and $1,320,000 to another called "Affordable Chicago Now!". Same day, both transfers.
  • "Elect Chicago Women" then spent $3,985,698 electing Melissa Bean in IL-08. "Affordable Chicago Now!" spent $4,379,243 electing Donna Miller in IL-02. Both PACs share the same repeat mega-donors on top of the UDP money.
  • The part you should read twice: "Elect Chicago Women" also transferred $1,000,000 to a third new PAC named "Chicago Progressive Partnership", which spent $663,879 attacking Junaid Ahmed, the actual progressive in the IL-08 race. An AIPAC-funded committee named itself "Progressive" to run ads against a progressive.
  • Our database figures reproduce: Miller's $4,393,893 matches Affordable Chicago Now's spending plus direct donations almost to the dollar; Bean's $4,716,060 is consistent with Elect Chicago Women's spending plus the affiliated committees.

Put the receipts together across states and the 2026 playbook is unmistakable: in high-profile races the lobby spends as itself (UDP in Michigan, Kentucky, Maryland), and in quieter primaries the same money arrives wearing a local costume: "MAGA KY" in Kentucky, "Bold America" in California, "Elect Chicago Women" and "Affordable Chicago Now!" in Illinois. Different names, same wire transfers, all traceable in the filings.

Caveats: processed FEC aggregates and itemized receipts as of August 20, 2026. Verify at fec.gov/data under committees C00936724, C00935049, and C00935809.

MD-05: $5.7M from AIPAC's super PAC for Steny Hoyer's open seat

Checked August 20, 2026 · Source: FEC independent-expenditure aggregates for Adrian Boafo (H6MD05321), 2026 cycle

Verdict: Confirmed. Reproduced within 0.4%.

Our database lists Maryland state delegate Adrian Boafo at $5,752,013. The FEC aggregates reproduce it: United Democracy Project spent $5,734,013 supporting Boafo in the primary for Steny Hoyer's open seat, plus small direct PAC donations (DMFI's direct spending shows $382). One committee, one candidate, nearly $6 million, in a safe-blue district where the primary is the election.

  • Boafo's other outside support: $5,259,858 from Protect Progress (the crypto-industry PAC, which we do not count as Israel lobby money), $600,000 from AmeriPAC, which is Hoyer's own leadership PAC, and smaller committees.
  • For scale: rival Harry Dunn's entire outside support was about $173K. No committee we found spent six figures for any other Democrat in the race.

Caveats: processed FEC aggregates as of August 20, 2026; totals grow as filings post. Verify at fec.gov/data under Boafo's independent expenditures.

CA-14: the shell chain, and a correction to our own database

Checked August 20, 2026 · Source: FEC independent-expenditure aggregates for Melissa Hernandez (H6CA14163) and FEC Schedule A receipts for Bold America (C00854299) and Project 218 (C00871368)

Verdict: Partially verified. We downgraded our own number.

Our database listed Hernandez at $6.4M, a figure published by an outside tracker. We tried to reproduce it from FEC filings and could not. Here's what the filings do show, and it's a story of its own:

  • Total outside spending supporting Hernandez: about $1.83M, from committees named Bold America ($664,921), Project 218 ($500,000), SD 2026 ($373,773), Pro-Choice Majority Action ($200,000), and For Our Values PAC ($86,447). No pro-Israel committee appears under its own name.
  • But follow the funding: Bold America took $1,000,000 directly from United Democracy Project, AIPAC's super PAC, in two transfers (May 1 and June 2, 2026), alongside money from intermediaries called "Democrats 2026" and "Proximo Action."
  • Project 218's money, $5.6M of it, came from a single entity called "Democracy Matters", which has no public donor trail we can find. Where that money originates, nobody can say from the filings.

So the honest statement is: at least $1M of AIPAC money reached the Hernandez race through a renamed intermediary, the full picture is deliberately obscured, and the $6.4M figure can't be confirmed from FEC records alone. Until it can, our database now lists Hernandez as "not listed" rather than repeat a number we can't stand behind. That's the standard: when we can't prove it, we don't print it, and when we got it wrong, we say so.

Caveats: PAC-to-PAC transfers make district-level attribution genuinely hard; more may surface in post-primary filings. Verify at fec.gov/data under committees C00854299 and C00871368.

Matt Dunlap (ME-02): is he taking Israel lobby money?

Checked August 13, 2026 · Source: FEC filings for Dunlap for Congress (C00922377), all reports through the July 2026 quarterly

Verdict: Clean. $0 found.

Dunlap launched his primary run attacking Jared Golden's AIPAC endorsement and Israel weapons votes. So we checked whether his own money is clean. We verified line by line, not inferred.

  • $0 from AIPAC, J Street, DMFI, Pro-Israel America, NORPAC, or any pro-Israel PAC or conduit.
  • Total raised cycle-to-date: ~$1.21M, overwhelmingly individual and ActBlue small-dollar money.
  • Total from ALL PACs and committees: $13,500, all five itemized: Free and Fair Democracy PAC ($5,000), National Nurses United ($3,000), Sara Gideon for Maine ($2,000), Pingree for Congress ($2,000), and Puget PAC ($1,500).
  • Conduit scan of every itemized individual receipt (~4,100 entries across four reports): only conduits are ActBlue and one Zinc Collective PAC earmark bundle ($12,233, a Democratic donor collective, not pro-Israel). Zero AIPAC or J Street earmarks.
  • Context: AIPAC money in ME-02 was on Golden's side ($1,071,069 career, per our database). Golden retired in November 2025. No United Democracy Project independent expenditures found in Maine this cycle.

Caveats: itemized data only covers donors over $200; small-dollar donors aren't traceable by anyone. Key filings at docquery.fec.gov: FEC-1993910 (Q2 2026), FEC-1989809 (pre-primary, amended), FEC-1961614 (Q1 2026), FEC-1939144 (Q4 2025).
